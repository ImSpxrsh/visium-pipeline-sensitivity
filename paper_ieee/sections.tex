\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A spatial transcriptomics study rarely admits a single ``correct'' analysis.
Between raw counts and a figure of spatial domains lie a dozen forks: how to
normalize, how many highly variable genes (HVGs) to keep, how many principal
components, whether the neighborhood graph is $k$-nearest-neighbor or
radius-based, whether clustering uses Leiden~\cite{traag2019louvain} or
Louvain~\cite{blondel2008fast} and at what resolution, and which test
declares a gene spatially variable~\cite{moran1950notes,
svensson2018spatialde, weber2023nnsvg}. Each fork has published precedent,
so each choice is individually defensible. Yet a study typically walks
\emph{one} path through this space and reports its endpoint as though it
were the destination.

This is the \emph{garden of forking paths}~\cite{gelman2013garden}: even
absent any intent to cherry-pick, the sheer number of reasonable analytic
choices means a reported result can be an artifact of the path taken rather
than a property of the underlying biology. \emph{Multiverse
analysis}~\cite{steegen2016increasing} and \emph{specification-curve
analysis}~\cite{simonsohn2020specification} respond to this by running
\emph{all} defensible analyses and reporting the distribution of
outcomes, an approach dramatically validated when 70 independent teams
analyzed one shared neuroimaging dataset and reached materially divergent
conclusions~\cite{botvinik2020variability}. Spatial transcriptomics has, to
date, lacked a systematic instrumentation of this problem: existing
benchmarks rank analysis \emph{methods} against a reference, but none
quantify how much a single analyst's reasonable choices move the
conclusions on a fixed piece of tissue.

We present MOSAIC, a specification-curve framework for spatial
transcriptomics. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{C1}] A parametric, bit-reproducible pipeline in which every
  analytic choice is a configuration variable, together with the
  \emph{Spatial Stability Score} ($S^3$), an interpretable family of
  stability metrics at the level of spots, genes, and headline
  summaries, with cluster labels made comparable across pipelines by
  optimal (Hungarian) assignment (\S\ref{sec:method}).
\item[\textbf{C2}] An empirical instability audit spanning two independent
  mouse-brain sections and a human tumor section, with formal statistical
  tests: permutation nulls, bootstrap confidence intervals, and a
  variance decomposition that identifies the single most consequential
  analytical choice (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\item[\textbf{C3}] Validation against independent, board-certified
  pathology, showing that analytical fragility concentrates precisely at
  the tumor \emph{margin} (the region of greatest clinical
  consequence), with the effect surviving a depth-stratified permutation
  control (\S\ref{sec:tumor}).
\item[\textbf{C4}] Two constructive uses of the multiverse beyond
  diagnosis: a co-association \emph{consensus} estimator that recovers
  ground-truth anatomy at least as well as the best single pipeline
  without needing the ground truth to select it, and a single-pipeline
  classifier that predicts multiverse fragility in advance
  (\S\ref{sec:consensus}--\S\ref{sec:downstream}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Multiverse and specification-curve analysis.}
Steegen~\emph{et al.}~\cite{steegen2016increasing} formalized the
multiverse and Simonsohn~\emph{et al.}~\cite{simonsohn2020specification}
the specification curve in psychology; Gelman and
Loken~\cite{gelman2013garden} articulated why analytic flexibility
threatens validity even without deliberate $p$-hacking, and
Botvinik-Nezer~\emph{et al.}~\cite{botvinik2020variability} demonstrated
the consequence empirically across 70 fMRI analysis teams. These works
motivate our approach but do not address spatial omics data or the
label-alignment problem that arbitrary cluster identities introduce.

\textbf{Spatial transcriptomics pipelines.} Standard analyses build on
SCANPY~\cite{wolf2018scanpy} and Squidpy~\cite{palla2022squidpy}, with
community detection via Louvain~\cite{blondel2008fast} or
Leiden~\cite{traag2019louvain,traag2011narrow} and spatially-variable-gene
(SVG) detection via Moran's $I$~\cite{moran1950notes},
SpatialDE~\cite{svensson2018spatialde}, or nnSVG~\cite{weber2023nnsvg}.
Reference atlases such as the DLPFC transcriptome~\cite{maynard2021transcriptome}
provide ground truth for benchmarking. Reviews of single-cell and spatial
integration methods~\cite{luecken2019current} typically rank competing
\emph{methods} against such a reference. Recent integration toolkits for
multi-platform spatial data, such as MOSAIK~\cite{baptista2025mosaik} (with
which our similarly-named framework should not be confused), unify data
sources but likewise do not quantify analytical instability. MOSAIC asks a
different question: holding the data fixed, how much does a competent
analyst's downstream conclusion move across the space of defensible
choices? We turn that instability signal into a spatially resolved,
actionable, \emph{constructive} output.

\textbf{Ensemble and consensus clustering.} Our co-association consensus
(\S\ref{sec:consensus}) builds on classical consensus
clustering~\cite{monti2003consensus} and cluster-ensemble
frameworks~\cite{strehl2002cluster}, which aggregate multiple partitions of
the \emph{same} method under resampling or initialization noise into a more
stable consensus. We instead aggregate partitions from a curated multiverse
of \emph{qualitatively different} analytical pipelines, not perturbations of
one method, and we are, to our knowledge, the first to show that such a
cross-pipeline consensus on real spatial transcriptomics data matches or
exceeds the best individual pipeline's agreement with ground truth
\emph{without access to that ground truth}, and to pair it with a
ground-truth-free per-spot confidence signal.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Specification space}
Each analytic stage is treated as an axis (Table~\ref{tab:space}). The full
Cartesian product exceeds 200 combinations; following specification-curve
practice, we evaluate a \emph{curated} set of \Nspecs{} pipelines spanning
canonical, conservative, aggressive, and extreme-but-defensible choices, so
the resulting multiverse remains interpretable as a matrix rather than an
unstructured search space.

\subsection{Parametric pipeline}
Given specification $s$, the pipeline maps raw counts to a clustered
\texttt{AnnData}~\cite{virshup2023anndata} object and an SVG table. Every
stochastic step is seeded (\texttt{random\_state}=42), and each
specification re-derives its normalized matrix from stored raw counts, so
no information leaks between specifications. Determinism is verified by
re-running a fixed specification and confirming bit-identical spot-level
labels across repeated runs.

\subsection{The Spatial Stability Score}
\label{sec:s3}
Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the specification set, $N=|\mathcal{S}|$, and
$c_s(i)\in\{1,\dots,K_s\}$ the cluster label of spot $i$ under $s$. Cluster
labels are arbitrary integers assigned independently per specification, so
$c_s$ and $c_{s'}$ are not directly comparable; we first align every
specification to a fixed reference $s_0$ via the overlap matrix
$M^{(s)}_{ab}=|\{i: c_{s_0}(i)=a \wedge c_s(i)=b\}|$ and solve for the
maximum-overlap injection
\begin{equation}
  \pi_s = \arg\max_{\pi} \sum_{b=1}^{K_s} M^{(s)}_{\pi(b),b}
\end{equation}
with the Hungarian algorithm~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian} in $O(K^3)$ time,
$K=\max(K_{s_0},K_s)$. Writing $\tilde c_s(i)=\pi_s(c_s(i))$, per-spot
stability is the frequency of the modal aligned label,
\begin{equation}
  S^3_{\text{spot}}(i) = \frac{1}{N}\max_{\ell}\left|\{s\in\mathcal{S}:\tilde
  c_s(i)=\ell\}\right| \in \left[\tfrac1N,1\right],
\end{equation}
per-gene stability is the fraction of specifications calling gene $g$ a
significant SVG,
\begin{equation}
  S^3_{\text{gene}}(g) = \frac1N\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}} \mathbb{1}[g\in\text{SVG}_s],
\end{equation}
and headline stability reports the mean, standard deviation, and range of a
scalar summary (e.g.\ domain count) across $\mathcal{S}$. As a label-free
complement, we also report the mean pairwise Adjusted Rand
Index~\cite{hubert1985comparing} between specifications, which requires no
alignment.

\subsection{Statistical validation}
\label{sec:stats}
Every positive claim in this paper is accompanied by a null and an
uncertainty estimate, not a point estimate alone.
\textbf{Permutation nulls} fix per-spot scores and permute region or
boundary labels ($B{=}1000$ draws, size-preserving), reporting a one-sided
empirical $p$-value with add-one correction. \textbf{Depth-stratified
nulls} additionally bin spots by sequencing-depth decile and permute
\emph{within} each bin, so the null preserves each group's true depth
distribution. This directly tests whether an effect is a sequencing-depth
artifact, instead of assuming random assignment controls for it.
\textbf{Bootstrap confidence intervals} resample spots with replacement
($B{=}1000$) to report 95\% CIs on effect magnitudes. \textbf{Variance
decomposition} regresses a headline summary on all analytical axes via
Type-II ANOVA, attributing variance share to each. \textbf{Confound checks}
correlate $S^3$ with sequencing depth to distinguish biological from
technical uncertainty.

\subsection{Complexity and architecture}
With $S$ spots, $H$ retained genes, $d$ PCA components, $P$ SVG
permutations, and $N$ specifications, per-specification cost is dominated
by SVG permutation testing, $O(SHd + PSH)$, giving sweep cost
$O(N(SHd+PSH))$; cross-specification alignment adds $O(NK^3)$, negligible
for $K\ll S$. Because specifications share no state, the sweep is
embarrassingly parallel across $W$ workers, $O(N(SHd+PSH)/W)$; sweeps use
content-hashed, idempotent checkpoints so re-running only computes new
specifications.

\section{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate on two independent 10x Genomics Visium mouse-brain coronal
sections distributed with Squidpy~\cite{palla2022squidpy} (a primary H\&E
section, \Nspots{} spots and \Ngenes{} genes; and, for generalization, an
independently prepared fluorescence section, 2{,}800 spots), both shipping
real histology and a 15-region curated anatomical annotation used as ground
truth. To test whether fragility tracks biological stakes, we additionally
run the full multiverse on the public 10x Visium human breast-cancer section
(\texttt{V1\_Breast\_Cancer\_Block\_A\_Section\_1}; invasive ductal
carcinoma; 3{,}798 spots), applying only minimal shared gene quality control
so every specification sees the same gene universe. For this section we use
per-spot board-certified pathologist annotations released with the SEDR
study, matched to Visium barcodes, as clinical ground truth; these labels
are produced entirely independently of any pipeline evaluated here. All
datasets are public and fetched programmatically; no data were generated for
this study.

\section{Results: Instability on Mouse Brain}
\label{sec:results}

\textbf{Headline instability.} Recovered spatial domains range from
\ClustMin{} to \ClustMax{} across the multiverse (mean
$\ClustMean{}\pm\ClustStd{}$). Label-invariant agreement is modest: mean
pairwise ARI between specifications is \AriMean{} (range
$\AriMin{}$--$\AriMax{}$). Different pipelines carve materially different
domains, not merely relabeled versions of one partition.

\textbf{Boundary fragility.} Per-spot $S^3$ concentrates instability at
region boundaries: \SpotStableFrac{} of spots are robust ($S^3\ge0.8$) and
\SpotFragileFrac{} fragile ($S^3<0.5$). Stratifying into boundary and
interior spots, mean $S^3$ is \BoundaryS{} at boundaries versus
\InteriorS{} in interiors, a gap of \StakesGap{} that a permutation null
rejects as chance ($p\,\StakesP{}$).

\textbf{Stability tracks biology, not technical noise.} Individual
specifications recover reference anatomy at mean ARI \RefAriMean{} (best
specification \RefAriMax{}); a permutation null confirms
\RegionsStable{}/\NregionsTotal{} anatomical regions are stable beyond
chance ($p<0.05$); and $S^3$ is essentially uncorrelated with sequencing
depth ($r=\ConfoundR{}$), so instability reflects biological rather than
technical uncertainty. (The co-association \emph{consensus} of
\S\ref{sec:consensus} improves on this per-specification agreement; here we
establish only that the individual pipelines are anatomically meaningful.)

\textbf{One choice dominates.} A Type-II variance decomposition attributes
\VarTopFrac{} of domain-count variance to clustering \VarTopAxis{} alone,
with every other axis contributing at most a few percent
(Fig.~\ref{fig:variance}), a concrete, actionable standardization
priority for the field.

\textbf{Gene-level and downstream churn.} Per-gene $S^3$ separates
consistently significant SVGs (\GeneRobustFrac{} with $S^3>0.8$) from those
whose significance flips with the analysis (\GeneFragileFrac{} with
$S^3<0.5$). The effect propagates downstream: a cross-validated classifier
predicting anatomical region from each specification's embedding swings
\DownstreamRangePP{} accuracy percentage points ($\DownstreamAccMin{}$--
$\DownstreamAccMax{}$) purely from upstream pipeline choice, and the
multiclass Brier score swings further still ($\BrierMin{}$--$\BrierMax{}$):
pipeline choice degrades \emph{calibration} more than accuracy
(Fig.~\ref{fig:downstreamml}).

\textbf{Generalization.} An independent fluorescence section reproduces a
comparable robust-core / fragile-tail $S^3$ distribution and pairwise ARI;
the pattern does not appear to be an artifact of one sample.

\section{Results: Higher-Stakes Human Tumor Tissue}
\label{sec:tumor}

Fragility persists on tumor tissue and is modestly \emph{larger}: mean
$S^3$ is \TumorBoundaryS{} at consensus-domain boundaries versus
\TumorInteriorS{} in interiors (gap \TumorGap{}, $p\,\TumorStakesP{}$),
compared to \StakesGap{} in brain. Tumor tissue introduces a genuine
technical confound absent in brain: $S^3$ correlates with sequencing
depth ($r=\TumorConfoundR{}$ versus $\ConfoundR{}$), so we test the gap
with the depth-stratified permutation null of \S\ref{sec:stats}. The gap
survives ($p\,\TumorDepthP{}$) and is not merely an artifact of
low-coverage spots.

\textbf{Independent pathology validation.} This section carries
independent, board-certified pathologist annotations (invasive carcinoma,
in-situ tumor, surrounding-tumor margin, healthy tissue). These labels let
us replace the consensus-domain boundary proxy used elsewhere with clinical
ground truth. Analytical fragility peaks precisely at the pathologist-defined
tumor \emph{margin}: mean $S^3$ is \PathMarginS{} at the margin versus
\PathInvasiveS{} in the invasive core and \PathHealthyS{} in healthy tissue
($p\,\PathP{}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:pathology}). A paired bootstrap (spot
resampling, $B{=}1000$) places the margin--core gap's 95\% CI at
$[0.128, 0.154]$, excluding zero, and the effect survives the
depth-stratified null ($p\,\PathDepthP{}$). The surgical margin (the
region of greatest clinical consequence, where a false boundary call could
misinform treatment) is exactly where analytical choices matter most, on
labels produced entirely independently of any pipeline in this study.

Finally, the dominant analytical axis does \emph{not} change with tissue
type: clustering resolution still explains the most domain-count variance
(\TumorVarTopFrac{}, versus \VarTopFrac{} in brain). This is an honest null
for the hypothesis that the most consequential choice is tissue-dependent,
reported plainly instead of reframed as a positive finding.

\section{The Multiverse as a Constructive Estimator}
\label{sec:consensus}

So far $S^3$ has served as a diagnostic. We show the multiverse also yields
a constructive output: a \emph{co-association} matrix (the fraction of
specifications placing each pair of spots in the same aligned cluster)
re-clustered into consensus domains. Single pipelines recover reference
anatomy at ARI \SingleWorstARI{}--\SingleBestARI{} (median
\SingleMedianARI{}); the consensus reaches \ConsensusARI{}, matching or
exceeding the best single pipeline near the reference granularity and
beating the median pipeline in \ConsensusBeatsMedianPct{} of consensus
granularity/linkage settings (Fig.~\ref{fig:consensus}). A researcher
cannot identify the single best pipeline without the ground truth they are
trying to recover; the consensus attains best-pipeline-or-better accuracy
using only the multiverse itself. Per-spot $S^3$ is additionally a
ground-truth-free confidence signal on the consensus: where the consensus
disagrees with reference anatomy, $S^3$ is significantly lower (mean $0.69$
vs.\ $0.81$; Mann--Whitney $p<10^{-40}$), and $S^3$ alone predicts consensus
correctness at ROC AUC \ConsensusUncAUC{} (consensus accuracy \ConsensusAcc{}).

\section{Predicting Fragility Before Running the Multiverse}
\label{sec:downstream}

A full \Nspecs{}-pipeline sweep is not always affordable. We show fragile
spots can be flagged from a \emph{single} pipeline: a cross-validated
logistic classifier, using only multiverse-free features from one run
(local transcriptomic heterogeneity, single-pipeline neighbor disagreement,
sequencing depth, and genes detected), predicts which spots the full
multiverse will find fragile ($S^3<0.5$) at ROC AUC \FragPredAUC{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fragpred}). The combined model improves on its strongest
single feature (single-pipeline neighbor disagreement, AUC $0.77$); depth
alone reaches only AUC $0.57$, so the prediction draws on genuinely local
structure and not on sequencing depth alone. A researcher can therefore
identify where a single result is least trustworthy, and decide whether the
full multiverse is worth running, \emph{before} committing to it.

\section{Practical Recommendations}
For researchers who cannot run a full multiverse, our variance
decomposition (Fig.~\ref{fig:variance}) gives a concrete priority order:
first, fix and report the clustering resolution, which accounts for
\VarTopFrac{} of domain-count variability, dwarfing every other choice;
second, report the neighborhood-graph size $k$. Normalization,
feature-selection method, HVG count, PCA dimensionality, and the
Leiden-versus-Louvain choice each move results only marginally. Finally,
treat SVG calls near the significance threshold as provisional and report
a per-gene $S^3$ alongside any SVG list.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
MOSAIC quantifies, rather than resolves, analytic ambiguity: it identifies
which conclusions are robust and which are contingent, and now, via the
consensus estimator, provides a route to a more accurate answer than any
single defensible choice. The agreement between per-spot $S^3$ and reference
anatomy, together with the near-zero depth correlation on brain tissue,
indicates $S^3$ measures biological rather than purely analytical
uncertainty. Limitations: our specification space is curated, not
exhaustive; we evaluate three Visium sections across two mouse-brain samples
and one human tumor, and a broader survey across further tissues, patients,
and sub-cellular platforms (e.g.\ Xenium) remains future work; where
pathologist annotations are unavailable we rely on a consensus-domain
boundary proxy, clearly labeled as such; and on tumor tissue, while the
depth-stratified null shows the boundary-fragility gap survives, we do not
claim a full multivariate deconfounding of every possible technical
covariate. The framework accommodates additional axes, datasets, SVG tests,
and stratifications without modification to its core architecture.

\textbf{Toward agentic multiverse navigation.} The curated \Nspecs{}-specification
set is a static sample of a far larger space. Because each specification
returns a well-defined stability signal, the multiverse is naturally framed
as a search problem: an agent could use Bayesian optimization or active
learning to propose specifications that maximally reduce uncertainty about
$S^3$, mapping the boundary between robust and contingent regions of the
configuration space more efficiently than uniform curation. The
embarrassingly parallel architecture makes such agent-driven exploration,
and eventually an exhaustive multiverse, computationally tractable.

\section{Conclusion}
We introduced MOSAIC, a specification-curve framework that turns the
garden-of-forking-paths problem in spatial transcriptomics into a
measurable, spatially resolved, and actionable signal. Beyond diagnosis,
we showed the multiverse itself is a better estimator than any single
pipeline, and that its fragility is predictable in advance from a single
run. Validated against independent pathology, the framework identifies
exactly the tissue regions (tumor margins) where the stakes of an
analytical choice are highest. This gives a concrete, reproducible tool for
auditing and improving spatial transcriptomics conclusions.

\section*{Reproducibility}
Every specification is seeded and re-derives its inputs from raw counts;
sweeps are idempotent and content-hashed, and a determinism check confirms
bit-identical spot-level labels on repeated runs of a fixed specification.
We release the pipeline code, the specification configuration, the aggregate
stability tables, statistical outputs, and figure-generation scripts at
\url{https://github.com/ImSpxrsh/MOSAIC-ISEF}. Analyses build on
NumPy~\cite{harris2020array}, SciPy~\cite{virtanen2020scipy},
scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011scikit}, SCANPY~\cite{wolf2018scanpy}, and
Squidpy~\cite{palla2022squidpy}.
